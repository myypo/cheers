# Visitor mode rules

How far a visual world may reach into a surface depends on its visitor mode. These rules govern every direction, composition, and mock for the surface; where they conflict with shared guidance, they win. Read the section for the surface's mode before writing directions in [new-work.md](new-work.md).

## Persuade and Experience

On these surfaces the design is what the visitor judges, so the world may own and structure the page.

### Directions

When the chosen world's source is itself an interface language, the surface may carry its native grammar across navigation, content, controls, and states.

For **Persuade**, the opening must make the offer intelligible and desirable, expose a clear action, and demonstrate something only this product can prove. The action is the one the category's visitors came to take, in its working form: an airline's route-and-dates search, a clinic's open appointment slots, a store's add to cart. A decorative link pointing at it elsewhere does not count. Conversion lives inside the form's own vocabulary: a hook that lands in one line, a visible primary action, a legible reading order. A committed form that hides the offer or the action has not finished translating.

For **Experience**, the work itself leads from the first viewport.

### Composition

The world may be the carrier of the page, never a picture of it: a facade, a shutter, or a book can be what the page is built on, while a viewport painted whole as a facade, book cover, or travel poster with no working parts is a picture of the world, not its website. Material coverage is wanted: the world's material is the ground and the major surfaces, its type sets the headlines. A world reduced to an accent on a neutral template is the category default with a sticker on it. Coverage is not performance: one dominant move acts out the concept, and the rest stays in the world's material without performing it.

Both ends erase the direction: the painted poster, and the generic product page (a headline over subcopy and a button, a row of equal cards below). A split hero (copy on one side, the product on the other, a badge row under it) or a centered headline over a row of cards is the template whatever world paints it.

The world carries the page's parts and never absorbs them: navigation set into a facade reads as links, copy on a shutter reads as set text, an action on a door plaque is plainly clickable, text reads against whatever sits behind it, sections keep a steady vertical rhythm, the primary action is unmistakable, and the section below starts as a section rather than more painting. Fix a poster by making its parts work where the world put them, never by moving them into a standard layout on the world's color.

When the world is an object, the page is that one object seen once, at one scale and from one point of view, and the object keeps its real form: a book opens to two pages on one gutter, never a third panel, and a spine that carries the navigation belongs to the book on the page, never a second book beside it. More content goes on the object's own surfaces or on the next spread below, never on extra copies or impossible folds.

Alternates each go further than the lead, never cleaner: a bolder use of the same world, a larger or stranger focal move, or a composition the category would not attempt.

### Depth

- **Type.** Write three concrete voice words with physical qualities (warm, mechanical, ceremonial, clinical, restless, tactile), never "modern" or "clean". Name the faces you would reach for by reflex and treat them as spent. Choose type like an object from the subject's world: museum caption, terminal manual, fabric label, concert poster, receipt, field guide. Fluid display sizing and strong scale contrast are available when the composition earns them. Light text on dark grounds needs more line height, a touch more tracking, and often one step more weight.
- **Color.** Committed, Full palette, and Drenched are available; do not hedge once chosen. Palette is voice: a calm brand and a restless one should not share palette mechanics. Name a real reference before picking values. If a cultural-symbol palette is obvious, reach past it and let type, imagery, and copy carry the cultural signal.
- **Layout.** Centered stacks are a default, not a concept. Asymmetry, strict visible grids, oversized type, and single-purpose sections are available when they match the world. Vary rhythm: the page can breathe, compress, pause, and accelerate.
- **Imagery.** Image-led subjects need imagery: restaurants, hotels, travel, fashion, products, communities, places. Use real project assets first; one decisive image beats five mediocre ones. Imagery can also be product screenshots, data visualization, authored SVG, canvas, or WebGL when that is the world. Alt text is part of the voice.
- **Motion.** Larger reveals, scroll moments, and typographic choreography are available, still with purpose, performance budgets, and reduced-motion alternatives. One memorable motion idea beats scattered micro-interactions.

## Operate

On an Operate surface the visitor came to get a job done, so the page is first a working product of its category and the world serves it.

### Directions

Someone who uses tools like this one should know where everything is before they notice the world. The world lends four things and only these: type, palette, density, and one signature move. It never supplies the layout, the navigation model, or the controls. Color does real jobs: a committed palette for the shell (a colored rail or header, a tinted ground), state colors, data series, selection, and the signature move. A gray screen with one accent color is the category default without the category's polish, not restraint.

A world taken from a tool or instrument the audience operates (a terminal, a scope, a control panel, a cockpit) or from a fictional interface comes back as a costume of that tool on a working screen: take from it only what transfers as type, palette, density, and a signature move, or replace it. Read every candidate and challenger through that limit before you fuse or judge it.

Write each direction's first viewport as the product's shell first: the navigation, the work areas and what each is for, the data the visitor reads first and its states, and then where the signature move lives. Expression may never obscure the task, state, or familiar affordance. Name the signature move concretely: service health drawn as a transit map, a schedule set in a timetable's column rhythm. A direction whose world shows up only as a color scheme has not found its move yet. When you weigh challengers, product clarity includes reading as a working product of this category.

### Composition

The screen sits in the direction's committed palette: its shell and navigation with a distinct area for each job and, where there is data, filters over what they scope and charts and tables with clear states. Every control is a standard web control the visitor already knows: buttons, tabs, inputs, selects, filters, tables with sortable headers, navigation that reads as navigation. Never a costume of the world's own interface: a terminal's function-key bar, an instrument's bezel, ruled ledger paper standing in for a table. A screen an engineer could not build from standard web components is a picture of the tool, not the tool. The finish bar is the best product in the category: an aligned grid, consistent spacing, a real type scale, and clear hierarchy. The signature move adds to a screen that is already that clean; it never stands in for it.

Use the screen's real content: the product name, realistic data with plausible values and units, real labels, and one state worth seeing (an alert, a degraded service, an empty filter result), never placeholder tiles.

Alternates vary the product's organisation, never its world coverage: the navigation model (side rail, top bar, command-first), how the work areas divide the screen, which data leads, the density, and where the signature move sits. Each must be recognizably different from the lead at thumbnail size; an alternate only its description can tell apart adds no choice. An alternate that adds world material, texture, or ornament goes further in the wrong direction.

Self-check: with the name covered, does it read as a product of its category, could the visitor do the task from it, and is it at least as clean as the category standard and more distinctive than it? If it reads as an artifact of the world first, rework it with the shell and work areas named region by region and the world held to its four contributions.

Depth for Operate surfaces lives in [operate.md](operate.md).

## Read

On a Read surface the visitor came to understand something, so the page is first a document of its kind, framed by the world.

### Directions

The reader's question leads, and comprehension and wayfinding stay intact at every step. Documentation reads as documentation, a guide as a guide, an article as an article, a changelog as a changelog. The world owns the frame and serves the column. The frame is the masthead, the navigation rail, the ground, the title and section openers, and the way code, tables, and callouts are set: there the world's palette, type, and signature move commit fully. The reading column inside it stays calm: a reading face, real contrast, a real measure, nothing performing behind the text. The world never supplies the reading order or replaces the navigation with something a reader has to learn.

Candidates from the world's own publishing traditions transfer best: reference books, type specimens, timetables, field guides, technical manuals, scholarly apparatus, a newspaper's wayfinding. Tools and fictional interfaces from the audience's world come back as costumes; take only type, palette, density, and a signature move from them.

Write each direction's first viewport as the document's shell first: the navigation and where the reader stands in it, the title and the opening that answers or frames the question, the reading column, and the way forward (contents, section headings, next and previous). Then name where the world owns the frame and where the signature move lives: a masthead, a colored rail, a running head, a margin system, a numbering scheme, a way of setting code or tables. When you weigh challengers, product clarity includes how fast a reader finds and understands the answer.

### Composition

A frame the world owns, in the direction's palette and type, around a calm reading column at a real measure (65–75 characters, body near 16px), with headings in a clear hierarchy and wayfinding that carries the reader through. Body text is set in a reading face; monospace is for code, parameters, and small labels, never running text or headings by default. Code, tables, notes, and figures sit in the forms readers already know, set in the world's type and palette. The signature move is one system used where it earns its place, never a frame repeated around every element.

Use the real material: the actual title, a real opening paragraph, real section names, and one example of the hardest content the surface holds (a code sample, a reference table, a diagram).

Alternates vary the reading structure and how the frame carries the world, never the column: the navigation model (sidebar tree, top tabs, inline contents), the column and margin system, reference density, and where the signature move appears. Each must be recognizably different from the lead at thumbnail size. Pushing world material into the reading column goes the wrong way.

Self-check: can a reader tell where they are and where to go next, and read the body comfortably? Then cover the name: could this be any other product's documentation? If so, push the frame further into the world. If it reads as an artifact of the world before it reads as a document, pull the world back to the frame.
