# New visual work

Use this flow for a new surface or a replacement visual identity. PRODUCT.md owns product truth. DESIGN.md owns durable visual decisions. A surface brief keeps strategy that belongs to one route or artifact. Complete [init.md](init.md) first when PRODUCT.md is missing; a missing DESIGN.md does not route back to init.

## 1. Decide what is already true

Read DESIGN.md, representative templates, CSS, tokens, components, and assets, and look at the running app when it is available.

- **Redesign:** preserve product truth, content, function, constraints, and explicit brand commitments; replace the old visual world rather than polishing it. The old look is evidence of what the subject is, not authority over what it becomes.
- **Established world:** inherit it. A missing DESIGN.md does not erase a coherent identity already in code; document that identity instead of inventing a replacement.
- **Incomplete brand:** preserve confirmed assets and recognizable traits, then expand the system with the user for this surface.
- **No visual authority:** create a new world with the user.

A section, component, feature, or state inside an established surface inherits that surface. Never turn a local addition into a new identity exercise.

## 2. Ask what will change the work

Before implementation, get the user's answer through the structured question tool when available; otherwise ask and wait. Ask two or three related questions; a precise request needs only a compact confirmation. Skip settled facts, not the confirmation: DESIGN.md settles the visual world, not this surface's purpose or concept.

- **Persuade:** who must act, what they should believe, which real proof, content, or assets earn that belief.
- **Operate:** the task, information, important states, frequency, constraints.
- **Read:** the reader's question, source material, structure, wayfinding.
- **Experience:** what leads, how exploration unfolds, which interaction or transition matters.

Across modes, ask what success looks like, what must remain untouched, and what would make a polished result feel wrong. Never ask for CSS values or canned aesthetic lanes.

If a confirmed [shape](shape.md) brief already answers these, use it instead of asking again.

## 3. Choose the right amount of invention

### Extend an existing surface

Inherit its world and composition. Resolve only the new purpose, content, hierarchy, states, interaction, and how the addition joins the surrounding experience. No direction round, and no DESIGN.md change unless the user approves a durable system change.

### Create a whole surface inside an established world

Keep the visual system fixed. Derive five to seven materially different structures from the content, task, and user behavior, ordered by resonance. Present three as options of equal weight, and make at least one of them come from the lower half of your list: your top-ranked structures are where every run in this category lands. Every option visualizes composition, not identity, because the world is settled. The user picks one, steers, or asks for a fresh three. Skip this round for a local extension or a precisely specified narrow request.

### Create or replace the visual world

1. Name the product's unique mechanism in one sentence, the audience's real scene, its cultural home, and what this first surface must prove. Note the page this category always ships and its predictable opposite; both are the rut and stay off the candidate list. A brief that paints its own picture (a product name, a titled artifact, a governing metaphor) adds its literal reading to the rut: spend at most one candidate on it.
2. From that cultural world, list seven concrete visual systems, artifacts, places, or rituals the audience knows by heart, each with one line on why it resonates and can carry the mechanism, ordered by resonance. The audience's world includes its graphic and screen traditions: the notation, publications, identity programs, data graphics, and interfaces it reads daily. On an Operate or Read surface, candidates never come from the tools and instruments the audience operates; a terminal or control panel handed to a working screen comes back as a costume. On a Persuade or Experience surface, also ask what this thing would look like as a physical object and what its world looked like before the web. Near-duplicates count once. When more than three of the seven share one material family, dig until the list spans at least three families.
3. Turn that material into complete directions: each joins a reusable visual world to a concrete first-surface experience, under the mode's rules in [modes.md](modes.md).
4. Break the ranking rut. Your top-ranked candidate is often where most runs in this category land, so it never leads by default. Lead with the strongest viable candidate from positions two to four of your list. Then deal two challengers: the most distant viable candidate from your list, and one foreign form, a working system from outside the audience's world (another culture's graphic tradition, another discipline's notation, an industry the category never borrows from) that can still carry the mechanism. Fuse each challenger before judging it: the challenger supplies the form and its system grammar, the product supplies every fact, clarity wins conflicts. Weigh each fused challenger against the lead on exactly two axes, audience identification and product clarity, as [modes.md](modes.md) defines clarity for this mode. Decide each verdict before any borrowing: **wins** (beats the lead on both; becomes the lead), **competitive** (holds one axis; stays a full alternate), or **declined** (loses both). Losing to strong grounded material is a valid outcome; beating a thin or one-family list is the point. A declined challenger still donates: name the one discipline of its system the lead lacks (a palette's total commitment, a grid's density courage, a form's structural honesty) and raise the lead to match, written as its own line naming the donor. Donations transfer ambition and discipline, never the challenger's clothes; a lifted motif is a costume note, not a raise, and one world owns the page.
5. Present one direction, fully committed and already raised: world, first viewport, visitor path, signature interaction, cross-surface reach, honest risk. Add winning or competitive challengers as full alternates (at most three) and declined ones as one-line notes with their verdict and what the lead kept from them. When your top-ranked candidate is not the lead, add it as one more option, labeled as your pick, with an honest risk line naming its familiarity when true: familiar and effective is a legitimate destination, and the user deciding that trade is the point of showing it. One pick, never a ranked list, and never in the lead position. Through the structured question tool, the option list carries the lead, the pick, the winning and competitive challengers, and the standing exit last; declined challengers fold into the lead's description as their kept lines.
6. Offer a re-roll with an optional one-line steer in three registers: plain (a fresh hand, same spread), safer (your remaining conventional grounded candidates, plus the category standard against named competitors), or bolder (foreign forms only, at full commitment). The register is the user's steering, never yours to pre-select. "Bolder" or "safer" said while a direction round is open means these registers, never the `bolder` or `harden` commands.

**The standing exit.** Every direction round offers one quiet, permanent alternative: the category standard, played straight. It is the user's door, never yours: never recommend it, never weigh it against the other directions, and never let it soften them. When the user takes it (the standing-exit option, a safer steer that lands there, or plain words asking for the familiar or competitor-like path), convention becomes the commitment: ask once for two or three products this should sit alongside, make their craft level the bar, and execute the convention at full fidelity, without irony or smuggled quirk. The anti-default checks in section 4 bind the unchosen default, not a chosen one. Record a standing preference as a brand commitment in PRODUCT.md.

A re-roll eliminates every direction already shown, grounded and challenger alike; after two consecutive re-rolls, ask what quality is missing. Re-roll on your own only on named factual grounds, when a direction cannot carry the product's truth or task; taste is never grounds. A user- or brief-pinned direction beats the round, always. Resolve collisions field by field: preserve every pinned constraint; in dimensions the brief leaves open, the chosen direction still binds through its topology, controls, state vocabulary, and ritual; when only its materials conflict with a pinned visual direction or a PRODUCT.md brand commitment, translate that material expression and name the translation. A look mismatch is not grounds to re-roll.

Every direction you present must already be viable: every relationship and claim it shows true, a real palette and component family, one product-specific experience, workable at full-surface scale within the available assets, tools, and performance budget. Truth binds claims, not demonstrations: in greenfield work, author whatever illustrative material the concept needs at full fidelity, label it synthetic wherever a visitor could mistake it for the real thing, and hand the user the list of what to replace. Prices, customers, benchmarks, endpoints, and capabilities the product does not have stay uninventable. Refusing a bold direction because its demonstration data does not exist yet is timidity wearing honesty's clothes.

If the harness can generate images and the user wants it, a mock of the chosen direction's first viewport may calibrate ambition before code. It is a reference for finish and commitment, not a pixel contract. Without one, the ambition lives in the direction contract's FIRST VIEWPORT block and signature interaction.

## 4. Commit the world

Pick a color strategy before picking colors: Restrained (neutrals tinted from the world plus one accent; common on screens people operate or read, where color still does real jobs), Committed (one saturated color carries 30–60% of the surface), Full palette (3–4 named roles), or Drenched (the surface is the color). Persuade and Experience surfaces have permission for the bolder strategies; take them when the brief allows. Color commits at page scale: fields that own whole regions, not accents scattered over a neutral ground. Dark or light is never a default: write one sentence of physical scene (who uses this, where, under what light) and let it force the answer.

Choose faces like objects from the subject's world, in the mode's register. Operate and Read surfaces are well served by system stacks and workhorse UI faces. Persuade and Experience surfaces want faces with a point of view, and these training-data defaults mean you stopped looking: Fraunces, Playfair Display, Cormorant, Lora, Crimson, Newsreader, Syne, Space Grotesk, Space Mono, IBM Plex, Inter-as-display, DM Sans, DM Serif, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, Instrument Sans. Naming one anyway requires a reason no other face could satisfy, and a subject association is never that reason: books wanting a serif and tech wanting a mono are the associations the list exists to break.

Calibration: generated interfaces cluster around a few looks regardless of subject: warm cream ground, high-contrast serif display, and a terracotta or signal-red accent; near-black with one neon accent and glowing edges; broadsheet-editorial hairlines, italic display serif, and small tracked mono labels. All are legitimate when the brief calls for them. Where the brief leaves the aesthetic free, landing in one means the self-check failed: if someone could guess your aesthetic from the category alone, or from category-plus-avoidance, rework it.

- Energy is not the enemy of trust. A brief's negative constraints (no gamification, no hype) rule out those devices, not exuberance, and adjectives describing the product's behavior (quiet support, calm coaching) do not dictate the surface's energy.
- Your rendition prior: warm, bookish, family, and child-facing subjects come out as cream grounds, italic serif display, and lamplight even when the direction never asked for them. Treat that first palette as already spent. Before writing code, reread the OWN-WORLD block: when it says cream, paper, parchment, ivory, or lamplight on a Persuade surface the brief did not pin, rework it from the world's saturated materials first. Book cloth, thread, jackets, and endpapers span the whole saturated spectrum; cream paper is its smallest corner.
- A brief-pinned world pins the world, not its softest rendition. Its full material range stays in play.

## 5. Record the decision

Before code, record the chosen direction under `## Direction contract` in the surface brief. Surface briefs live at `.cheers-design/surfaces/<slug>.md`; compute the slug from the primary target's source path with `node <skill-dir>/scripts/critique-storage.mjs slug "<target>"`. Read an existing brief before updating it.

Use six short blocks, roughly 150 words total:

- **THESIS:** the one idea this surface owns and the category-default arrangement it refuses.
- **OWN-WORLD:** the palette and component language, specific enough to be recognizable with all content removed.
- **STORY:** what the visitor understands, believes, and does.
- **FIRST VIEWPORT:** the exact composition, what is where and at what scale, and where the primary action sits.
- **FORM:** the chosen form, its position on your ordered list, the signature interaction, and the motion grammar.
- **FINISH:** verbatim "unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, and DESIGN.md".

If a block reads like a mood, the direction is not decided yet. Keep the rest of the brief small: scope and visitor mode; audience, job, action or task, proof or content, constraints; memorable moment; unresolved decisions. Do not copy global product truth or DESIGN.md tokens into it. Re-read the brief after writing and confirm all six blocks are present.

Never copy the direction contract into Rust source, templates, CSS, or anything delivered to the browser: no rendered comments, hidden DOM, `data-*` attributes, metadata, or accessibility-only text. Reviewers and documenters receive it from the surface brief.

On a new or replacement world, DESIGN.md is written at finish from the built world (section 7); a rulebook written before the build gets defended against reality instead of describing it. An ordinary extension does not rewrite DESIGN.md.

For `shape`, return the selected direction to [shape.md](shape.md) and stop before persistence or implementation.

## 6. Build with full commitment

Build the chosen direction, not a safer interpretation of it. The form supplies structure, reading order, component conventions, and native motion; the product supplies every fact. Commit every atom: nav, buttons, inputs, and links are rebuilt in the form's vocabulary, and a stock component inside a committed form is a lapse. Land the first build fully committed; the passes that follow make it clear and effective, never diluted.

1. **Structure before details.** Plan in design terms: landmarks and heading hierarchy, content groups and reading order, primary, secondary, and tertiary actions, form, table, list, detail, empty, and error patterns, refreshable regions as conceptual boundaries, and the mobile structure (not a squeezed desktop). Build semantic shape first; never begin by styling a pile of divs.
2. **Implement through `cheers`.** Use it for `Render`, generated helpers, Datastar attributes, actions, patches, streams, tests, and formatting. From this skill, keep interaction honest and backend-confirmed, pending feedback visible without implying success, local affordances local, state and errors near the decision point, components extracted when they clarify repeated UI, and CSS in the project's conventions and token vocabulary.
3. **Apply the craft floor.** [craft-floor.md](craft-floor.md) is loaded by now; build to it without announcing it.

Across the build:

- **The first viewport is a thesis, not a header.** Demonstrate the mechanism immediately, at the scale the form has in life; do not trap the concept inside a standard hero or card shell. The memory test: if someone left after one viewport, what would they describe an hour later? If the honest answer is a mood, the concept has not committed yet.
- **Prove, don't claim.** Show the subject doing its job: the interface at work, the mechanism dramatized, specifics a competitor could not copy-paste.
- **Author the assets; never substitute chrome.** Great surfaces live on carefully made content: names, entries, copy, covers, thumbnails, textures. Every blank the ask round left open is yours to author at production fidelity. Gradients, glass, generic icon tiles, and many-vertex `clip-path` polygons where an authored asset belongs are the gap wearing chrome.
- **Build the form's web leverage.** When the chosen world names a technique (canvas, WebGL, view transitions, generative motion), build the technique itself, not a static imitation, within the JS-is-exceptional guardrail.
- **Pace the scroll like a studio.** Vary density, scale, image, motion, and quiet inside one grammar; a dense passage earns a quiet one, and the page ends anchored by a real close.
- **Use real, verified imagery when the brief implies it.** Search for the subject's physical object rather than the category; verify stock URLs resolve.
- **Author motion as material.** Give the page the form's native motion once, orchestrated, rather than scattered hover effects. Bound expensive effects and keep content visible by default.

Preserve semantics, accessibility, performance, responsiveness, project conventions, and working behavior.

## 7. Inspect and finish

Inspect in one batched round: desktop (1440 wide) and mobile (390 wide) captures of the running app, through browser automation when available. Headless Chromium (`chromium --headless --screenshot=<file> --window-size=1440,900 <url>`) is a fallback that captures only the window, so raise the window height to cover the page. Add the user's actual viewport when known. Settle or disable entrance motion first, capture full pages from the document top, then open every capture once and confirm it shows what its name claims: no blank regions, no wrong section, no half-loaded state. Save them as `desktop.png` and `mobile.png` in `.cheers-design/review/<slug>/`, one directory per changed page, using the same slug helper as surface briefs. Clear stale captures from that directory before capturing.

Critique the render against the request and the direction contract, fix material gaps in one batch, and confirm with one final round. Two rounds is the ceiling; after the second, the build thread's polishing is over, and whatever remains goes through the reviewer, where fresh context finds it better. Do not invent defects to show iteration. Run the [mechanical scan](../SKILL.md#mechanical-scan-optional) once over the changed pages, fix what is mechanical, and pass the rest to the reviewer. On a Persuade surface, confirm a first-time visitor knows what this is, why it matters, and what to do within seconds.

Then hand off to a fresh reviewer. When the harness can spawn subagents, spawn one with no inherited conversation and have it follow [finish-review.md](finish-review.md), passing the original request, confirmed answers, the changed files, the capture paths, the surface brief path, the PRODUCT.md path, any scan findings, and the craft-floor path. A reviewer that inherits your transcript inherits your optimism. If an approved image mock exists, pass it as a critique reference: provocation about what it dared, not a spec. Check the return carries the disposition line and its contracted sections; on an empty or thrashed return, respawn once with the same inputs. Without subagents, step fully out of the build context, run finish-review.md yourself, write its full output before acting on it, and disclose the substitution in one line at the end.

Act on the reviewer's disposition:

- **recapture:** the evidence failed, not the build. Recapture what it names and run a full review again.
- **rebuild:** fidelity failed wholesale. Skip the fix batch: re-derive the named regions, produce the named assets, and send the result for a fresh full review, never a verdict pass. Tell the user what is happening; consult them only on a second rebuild or when rebuilding would discard content they approved.
- **fix:** apply the material fixes in one batch, recapture the same files, and send them back to the same reviewer (by continuation when the harness supports it, otherwise a fresh subagent running finish-review.md's Verdict pass) to score each fix resolved, partial, or unresolved. Partial or unresolved fixes get another batch, recapture, and verdict. Two rounds is the unattended budget; in an attended session, when the second verdict still lists open items, put the table in front of the user and let them choose between shipping as it stands and funding another round. Stop the moment a round resolves nothing. Work only from the reviewer's list, never your own re-opened hunt.
- **ship:** nothing is owed.

Report the verdict under the reviewer's own word and at its actual scope. A verdict pass that scored three fixes resolved supports "all three fixes resolved", not "no issues remain". When the user answers a ship with contrary evidence, that evidence outranks your captures: start a fresh full review with it.

After the last correction, record the world. New worlds and approved system changes require a token-bearing DESIGN.md written from the build per [document.md](document.md), preferably by a fresh subagent given the project root, changed files, direction contract, PRODUCT.md, and document.md. Never canonize a craft-floor refusal into DESIGN.md; note it as a defect the build carries. Ordinary extensions compare the finished build against the incumbent system, preserve its files, and report pre-existing drift without repairing it unasked.

Once review and documentation are complete, append `FINISHED: <disposition> <YYYY-MM-DD>` under the direction contract in the surface brief. A brief without that line is an unfinished build.
